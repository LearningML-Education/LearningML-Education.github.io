The Internet, as Jesus Moreno appreciates in this [tweet](https://twitter.com/J_MorenoL/status/1274789026428522500), has wonderful things. Since we started to spread LearningML the story that the [tweet](https://twitter.com/J_MorenoL/status/1274789026428522500) tells has been repeated 3 times.

First, after speaking at an **[online seminar](https://web.learningml.org/en/learningml-in-the-biweekly-seminars-of-the-raspberry-pi-foundation/) on Computing Education Research** organized by the Foundation [Raspberry Pi](https://www.raspberrypi.org/), **Dave Ames (@davidames)** offered to **translate** the messages of our application **into English.**

Then, during the [online research](https://web.learningml.org/en/learningml-online-research/) we have recently done, one of the participants, **María Loureiro (@tecnoloxia),** offered to **translate LearningML into Galician.**

And finally the story of the [tweet](https://twitter.com/J_MorenoL/status/1274789026428522500) happened: from a meeting about [Scratch](https://scratch.mit.edu/) organized by [ScratchCatalà](https://www.scratchcatala.com/), the teachers , **Vicenç Casas (@VicenCasas) and David Muns (@davidmuns)** offered to do the **translation into Catalan.**

So in less than a month and thanks to the power of the Internet collaboration, we have had LearningML available in Spanish, English, Galician and Catalan. The process, as Vicenç Casas (@VicenCasas) points out in this [tweet](https://twitter.com/VicenCasas/status/1275341224623443969), is very simple. **And you, do you dare to translate LearningML to another language?**
